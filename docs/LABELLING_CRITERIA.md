### 1.2b The singular/plural surname rule — founder ruling 2026-08-30
*"A guy can be named Jim Crane but not Jim Cranes. Nobody is named a plural."*

A handful of equipment words are also plausible **surnames** in the singular:
`CRANE · LOADER · GRADER · DOZER · QUARRY · MIXER`.
These qualify **only** when the string proves it is a firm, by any one of:
- the equipment word is **plural or possessive-plural** — `BOBS CRANES`, `RMS CRANES`;
- a **corporate marker or digit** is present — `EAGLE CRANE, LLC`;
- **another token is possessive** — `JIMS CRANE` (the *s* is on the owner);
- a **second equipment word** is present — `CRANE & RIGGING`.

Otherwise the row reads as a person and is rejected: `CRANE, ROBERT GALE`, `LOADER DINAH P`.

**Words nobody is ever named qualify in every form** — *"nobody in the history of the world has the
last name Excavation"*: `EXCAVATION · EXCAVATING · DEMOLITION · CONCRETE · PAVING · EARTHMOVING`…
This is why `DERRICK` and `AUGER` had to be deleted outright: they are surnames whose plural
never appears.

**Route A is independent of all of this.** If the lender is a named manufacturer or dealer, the
borrower's name is irrelevant — Terex sells cranes, so `BOBS COOKIES ← TEREX` is in scope. If the
lender is a bank and the borrower name carries no equipment category, **neither qualifier is met
and the row does not get in**.

### 1.2 Word-form allowances (the "exemption logic")
A word matches in **any** of these forms:
- **singular or plural** — `CRANE` = `CRANES`; `EXCAVATOR` = `EXCAVATORS`
- **possessive** — `BOB'S CRANES` = `BOBS CRANES` (the apostrophe is stripped before matching)
- **possessive plural** — `SHIFT'S EXCAVATORS` = `SHIFTS EXCAVATORS`
- **-es plural** where the word ends in ch/sh/x/z/s
- **spaced or joined multiword** — `SITE WORK` = `SITEWORK`; `EARTH MOVING` = `EARTHMOVING`
Matching is **whole-word only**. `CRANE` does not match `CRANEBROOK`. `DEMO` does not match
`DEMOGRAPHIC`. `CONCRETE` does not match `CONCRETION`.

# PART 3 — THE NAME FIELD.

## 3.1 How a name is read
The name field is **one string, split on spaces**. Each space breaks a segment.

## 3.2 Person identity — order does not matter, middle initials do not matter
**`HOWARD JOHN F` and `JOHN HOWARD` are the same man.** So are:
- `SMITH JOHN` = `JOHN SMITH`
- `SMITH JOHN A` = `JOHN SMITH` = `SMITH JOHN`
- `HASART JEROLD G` = `JEROLD HASART`

**Rule: drop any single-letter token, then compare the remaining words in any order.** A middle initial
is not legally required on a filing, so its presence or absence is never a difference.

**But different given names are different people:**
- `WOOD DONNA L` vs `WOOD DONALD J` → **DIFFERENT people** (Donna ≠ Donald)
- `LEY HENRY JR` vs `LEY BARBARA` → **DIFFERENT people**
- `TALBOTT HARRY B` vs `TALBOTT HARRY C` → **same first and last, differing only by middle initial →
  SAME person**

## 3.3 Person or company? — decisive tests, in order
**It is a COMPANY if any of these hold:**
1. **A corporate suffix is present** — `INC` · `LLC` · `LLLP` · `LLP` · `LP` · `CORP` · `CORPORATION` ·
   `CO` · `COMPANY` · `LTD` · `LIMITED` · `PLLC` · `PC` · `NA` · `TRUST` · `PARTNERSHIP`.
   **This is decisive however person-like the stem reads.** `STUTSMAN-GERBAZ, INC.` is a company.
2. **A dash joins two surnames** — `STUTSMAN-GERBAZ`, `SMITH-JONES`. **No person is named
   "Spencer-William."** A hyphenated *given* name (`MARY-ANNE SMITH`) is still a person.
3. **A trade or equipment word appears** — `HERNANDEZ EXCAVATING`, `OMARS CONSTRUCTION`,
   `CARLOS EXCAVATION`. Even where the stem is plainly a surname, the trade word makes it a business.
4. **A possessive or plural of a personal name** — `COHEN'S`, `COHENS`, `SPENCERS`, `O'BRIANS`,
   `BOBS`, `JIMS`. **`O'BRIAN` is a person; `O'BRIANS` is a business.**
5. **A digit appears** — `4 M FEEDERS`, `160 CORP`.
6. Words like `ENTERPRISES` · `INDUSTRIES` · `GROUP` · `HOLDINGS` · `SERVICES` · `SYSTEMS` ·
   `SOLUTIONS` · `ASSOCIATES` · `PARTNERS` · `VENTURES` · `PROPERTIES`.

**Otherwise treat it as a person** — two or three tokens, no suffix, no trade word, no digit.

## 3.4 Entity type IS identity
**`LLC` ≠ `INC` ≠ `CORP` ≠ `LP`.** `ACME EXCAVATING LLC` and `ACME EXCAVATING INC` are **two records**,
not one, and the pair is a real judgement call — not a formatting artifact.

## 3.5 What is NOT a name difference
- **Case** — `Kubota Credit Corporation` = `KUBOTA CREDIT CORPORATION`
- **Punctuation** — `WAGNER EQUIPMENT CO.` = `WAGNER EQUIPMENT CO`; `L.L.C.` = `LLC`;
  `FARM'S` = `FARMS`
- **`&` and `AND`** — `SMITH & SONS` = `SMITH AND SONS`
- **Abbreviation of the same word** — `COLO` = `COLORADO` · `CONST` = `CONSTRUCTION` ·
  `BROS` = `BROTHERS` · `EQUIP` = `EQUIPMENT` · `MFG` = `MANUFACTURING` · `INTL` = `INTERNATIONAL` ·
  `NATL` = `NATIONAL` · `U S` = `US`
- **Truncation** — `COLORADO BANK AND TRUST CO OF LA JUN` = `… LA JUNTA`
- **Obvious typo / OCR damage** — `AMEERICAN`, `AMERICAN ATIONAL`, `NATIONAL BANJ`, `INDPENDENCE`,
  `CORPROATION`, `TELLRIDE` all read as their correct spelling
- **Spacing** — `BLU SKY` = `BLUSKY` · `K R SWERDFEGER` = `KR SWERDFEGER` · `4 K FARMS` = `4K FARMS`

# PART 4 — THE ADDRESS FIELD. Full expansion tables.

**Principle: an address matches if the STREET NUMBER is the same and every abbreviation expands to the
same word.** Formatting is never an address difference.

## 4.2 Directions
`N` = NORTH · `S` = SOUTH · `E` = EAST · `W` = WEST ·
`NE` = NORTHEAST · `NW` = NORTHWEST · `SE` = SOUTHEAST · `SW` = SOUTHWEST
Applies before **and** after the street name: `1200 S TOWNSEND` = `1200 SOUTH TOWNSEND`;
`AVE E` = `AVENUE EAST`.

## 4.4 Units, suites and boxes
| short | long |
|---|---|
| STE | SUITE |
| APT | APARTMENT |
| BLDG | BUILDING |
| FL | FLOOR |
| RM | ROOM |
| DEPT | DEPARTMENT |
| UNIT, `#` | UNIT |
| PO BOX | P.O. BOX, P O BOX, POST OFFICE BOX |

`#130` = `STE 130` = `SUITE 130` = `UNIT 130`. A suite number present on one record and absent on the
other is **not** a different address when the street number matches.

## 4.5 Ordinals
`1ST` = `FIRST` · `2ND` = `SECOND` · `3RD` = `THIRD` · `4TH` = `FOURTH` … `129 SOUTH 3RD STREET` =
`129 S THIRD ST`.

## 4.6 What IS a real address difference
- **A different street number** — `4475 E 50TH AVE` vs `4475 E 70TH AVE` are two addresses
- **A different street name** — `TOMAH RD` vs `MONACO ST`
- **A different PO Box number** — `PO BOX 305` vs `PO BOX 210`
- A street address on one record and a PO Box on the other **is** a different address (though the same
  firm may legitimately use both — that is R4)

# PART 5 — ZIP, STATE AND CITY.

- **ZIP+4 is not a different ZIP.** `80110-2109` = `801102109` = `80110`
- **A ZIP that disagrees while the name and street agree is a DATA ERROR — ignore it.**
  `1740 BROADWAY, DENVER 81137` and `1740 BROADWAY, DENVER 80274` are the same address
- **A wrong state code on an otherwise-consistent record is a DATA ERROR — ignore it.** `CT` on a record
  whose city, street and ZIP are plainly Colorado
- **City spelling variants are not a difference** — `COLORADO SPR` = `COLORADO SPRINGS`;
  `GRAND JCT` = `GRAND JUNCTION`; `HENDE` = `HENDERSON`
- **Genuinely different cities ARE a difference** — Denver vs Pueblo
- **A blank field is never evidence.** Decide on the fields that are present

# PART 6 — QUESTION 1: ENTITY — "same firm?" → SAME / DIFFERENT / UNSURE

Read the two **names** first. About 90% decide from names alone. Consult the address only when the names
are identical or near-identical.

**Step 1 — clean the fields** using Parts 3.5, 4 and 5. Formatting, typos, abbreviations, data errors
and blanks are resolved *before* any rule is applied.

**Step 2 — apply in order. Lower number wins.**

| # | rule | → |
|---|---|---|
| **R0** | identical name AND identical address | **SAME** |
| **R1** | plainly different firms, no shared address *(commonest case)* | **DIFFERENT** |
| **R2** | one name is a typo / truncation / abbreviation of the other. **Tested BEFORE R6** — a spelling variant is not a different name | **SAME** |
| **R3** | suffix-only difference — LLC vs INC vs CORP | **SAME** |
| **R4** | same name, same city, different address — moved, or a yard and an office. Different PO Box numbers count | **SAME** |
| **R5** | same name, **different cities**, nothing else shared | **DIFFERENT** |
| **R6** | **DIFFERENT NAMES AT THE SAME ADDRESS.** Registered agents, franchise HQs, office buildings, virtual offices and family properties all put unrelated filers at one address. **ONE exception:** numbered outlets of one chain (`COUNTRY HARVEST BUFFET 103` vs `500`) are SAME — the *name* says so, not the address | **DIFFERENT** |
| **R7** | distinct legal entities of one corporate family — `CATERPILLAR FINANCIAL` vs `CATERPILLAR INC`; `X, A DIVISION OF Y` vs `Y`; Farm Credit `PCA` vs `FLCA` are separate charters | **DIFFERENT** |
| **R8** | two **different** person-names at one address — spouses and relatives are different borrowers. The **same** person twice is SAME | **DIFFERENT** |
| **R9** | successor / renamed entity — `NORWEST BANK COLORADO` vs `WELLS FARGO`. We label records as filed, not corporate history | **DIFFERENT** |
| **R10** | undecidable after all of the above | **UNSURE** |

**R2 is a cleaning step, not a competing verdict.** It establishes whether the names are the same; it
cannot outrank R5, whose premise it produces. Its stated priority is over **R6** only.

**UNSURE means the RULE does not decide** — not "I feel uncertain." If the rule gives an answer, give it
even when it feels wrong. Target under 5%.

# PART 7 — QUESTION 2: PREMISES — "one operation?" → ONE-OP / SEPARATE / UNSURE

These are **different names at the same address**. You already know they are different *firms*. The
question is whether they are **one operation** — one yard, one farm, one family business buying equipment
under more than one name.

**The test: would ONE lender assessing exposure at this address want BOTH loans?**

| **ONE-OP** | **SEPARATE** |
|---|---|
| A married couple who co-signed — `WOOD DONNA L` / `WOOD DONALD J` at one PO box | A registered agent's address |
| A person and their own company at one address | A franchise headquarters |
| Related SPEs at one office — `LEAD PROPERTIES II` / `III` | An office building or business park |
| `LBC INC` / `LBC LANDSCAPING LLC` — the names tie them | A mall unit |
| Literally the same party under a name-order variant (`HOWARD, JOHN F` / `John F. Howard`) — mark ONE-OP and note it | A virtual office with hundreds of tenants |
| A parent and a subsidiary trading from one yard | Unrelated businesses that merely share a mailbox |

**If one of them looks defunct and the other active, that is still SEPARATE** unless the names tie them
together. **Record the loan year** — it is what distinguishes a live operation from a lapsed one.

# PART 8 — DUPLICATES vs LOAN HISTORY.

**Same identity + same address = ONE ENTITY**, however many times it was filed.
**But different filing numbers or different lien dates are NOT duplicates — they are the same party with
several machine loans.** Collapse the identity; keep every loan.

`SEMA CONSTRUCTION, INC.` at one address with 338 filings from 2005 to 2026 is **one entity with a
338-loan history**, not 338 records and not one record with 337 thrown away. That history is the entity
timeline, the refinancing signal and the lender relationship all at once.

# PART 9 — WHAT NOT TO DO
- **Do not look anything up.** No Google, no Secretary of State search. Outside research makes the labels
  unreproducible and is not available at scale, so a reviewer discounts the whole number
- **Do not revisit an earlier row** after seeing a later one
- **Do not compare across regions.** Colorado and Connecticut are separate registers
- **Do not leave a row blank.** Every row gets exactly one label
- **Do not guess to avoid UNSURE** — but do not reach for UNSURE to avoid deciding either
